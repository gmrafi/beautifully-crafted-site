# Algorism Press website

## Direction
Create an English-only publishing website using your supplied logo and text. The design will feel like a carefully typeset academic publication: crisp off-white pages, deep emerald accents, restrained blue details drawn from the logo, large editorial headings, fine rules, and generous space.

## Pages and content
- **Home:** prominently display “Algorism Press” and “Code to Print, Research to Global Books,” introduce the publishing vision, feature your logo, and provide clear links to publications and institutional inquiries. Follow with an overview of structure, clarity, and continuity.
- **Vision & Philosophy:** present the book as a built object, the connected system of ideas and pages, and the Algorism Standard using your supplied copy.
- **What We Do:** explain manuscript structure, readable presentation, publication preparation, and the four-stage working process.
- **Publications:** show an honest “Coming soon” state. Do not invent book titles, availability, partnerships, or distribution claims.
- **Institutional & Contact:** present the faculty and university collaboration invitation, your supplied email, phone number, location, and GitHub link. Inquiry buttons open an email addressed to your supplied contact; no pretend submission form.
- **Shared navigation and footer:** provide access to every page, show the current development status, editorial divisions, copyright, and your supplied design credit. Include a compact navigation menu on small screens; omit the language switch.

## Logo and visual treatment
- Use the uploaded AP logo unchanged, preserving its proportions and making it a visible part of the first screen.
- Create the browser icon from the same logo.
- Combine editorial serif headings with a clear sans-serif body font, book-like typography, and subtle motion that respects reduced-motion preferences.
- Keep layouts readable on phones and computers, with visible links, clear contrast, and no overlapping text.

## Technical details
- Replace the current home-page placeholder and use separate TanStack pages for the named content areas.
- Use shared navigation, footer, and reusable presentation elements; define the palette as semantic design tokens.
- Store the uploaded logo through Lovable Assets, with a separate real favicon file.
- Add unique page titles, descriptions, and social metadata to each content page.
- Keep this scope presentation-only: no accounts, payments, database, or publication-management system.

## Verification
Check every navigation link, the small-screen menu, email and phone actions, logo rendering, and publication status. Review the website at desktop and phone sizes and run relevant existing tests before completion.
